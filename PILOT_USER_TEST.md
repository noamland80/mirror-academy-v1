# MIRROR Academy — the validation test, before any efficacy claim

**Status: not yet run. No claim about whether this product improves consultations is supportable until it has been.**

Everything verified so far is internal: the content is structurally complete, the
branching is real, the citations match the book, the Spanish is at parity, and
1,500+ assertions pass. None of that is evidence that a practitioner consults
differently on Tuesday. This document defines the smallest test that would be.

---

## Who

Four people, one clinic, one week. Not a focus group — four people using the
product for real and being watched while they do it.

| | Who | Why this person |
|---|---|---|
| **P1** | A practitioner in her first year | Finds out whether the Academy can teach someone with no method at all |
| **P2** | A practitioner with 5+ years who converts well | The hardest audience: if she finds nothing here, the product is a training aid for juniors and should be priced as one |
| **P3** | A practitioner who converts poorly and knows it | The person the product is actually for. Watch whether she feels taught or judged |
| **M1** | The clinic owner or manager | She is the buyer. Her question is not "is this good" but "would I make my team do this" |

Recruit from **one clinic**, not four. The manager view only means anything when
the practitioners in it are her own, and the commercial question is whether a
clinic adopts this, not whether an individual likes it.

---

## Setup

- Each person gets their own account, created by M1 through the invitation flow.
  Do not pre-seed their progress. The empty state is part of what is being tested.
- P1 and P3 work in English or Spanish as they prefer; **at least one person must
  run the whole test in Spanish.** Parity is a claim; this is where it gets tested.
- Sessions are observed, not reported. Screen-share or sit beside them. What
  people say afterwards about software is not reliable; where they stop moving is.

---

## The tasks

### Session 1 — first 30 minutes, each practitioner, observed

Give them the login and one sentence: *"This is consultation training. See what it is."* Nothing else. Then watch and time:

| # | Task | What is being measured |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sign in and reach a first lesson without being told how | Whether onboarding works or is skipped |
| 2 | Complete Module 1, Lesson 1 | Time to the first decision. **If she is still reading after 90 seconds, the lesson has failed** |
| 3 | Answer a `choose` block wrongly and meet the RETRY stage | Does she understand she is being given a second attempt, or does she think she broke something? |
| 4 | Open the case library and pick a case unprompted | Which case she picks, and why. If everybody picks Case 01 because it is first, the library is not doing its job |
| 5 | Run one full consultation to a derived outcome | Total time. Where she hesitates. Whether she reads the MIRROR feedback or clicks past it |
| 6 | Operate one Toolkit and hit its phase gate | Does the gate read as the product teaching her, or as the product blocking her? |

**Record for each:** time on task, every place she scrolled back, every place she
went silent, every place she asked a question, and every place she clicked
"continue" without reading.

### Session 2 — the real consultation, 3–7 days later

Each practitioner completes one module's **field assignment** in an actual
consultation, then answers the field-journal question in the product.

This is the only task that tests the product's actual promise. Collect:
- her field-journal answer, verbatim;
- whether she can name the sentence she said differently because of the Academy;
- whether the client did anything she did not expect.

### Session 3 — the manager, 30 minutes, after Session 2

M1 opens the coaching centre cold, having seen her team's real activity, and is
asked to do three things without help:
1. Name which practitioner she would coach first, and why.
2. Open one consultation and say what went wrong in it.
3. Say what she would ask that practitioner to repeat.

**The product succeeds here only if her answers come off the screen.** If she
answers from what she already believed about that person, the coaching centre has
not earned its place.

## What counts as a pass

Set these before running it, so the result cannot be argued into a success.

| Claim | Passes only if |
|---|---|
| A practitioner can start unaided | 3 of 3 reach a first decision within 3 minutes with no help |
| Lessons teach rather than explain | 3 of 3 can state, unprompted, one thing they would now say differently |
| The retry stage works | At least 2 of 3 recognise it as a second attempt without asking what happened |
| Cases feel like people | 3 of 3 describe at least one client as a person ("she was defensive"), not as an exercise |
| It transfers to a real room | **At least 2 of 3 name a specific sentence they used in an actual consultation** |
| Spanish is a first-class product | The Spanish-language tester reports no moment where she switched to English to understand something |
| The manager view is worth money | M1's coaching decision comes from the screen, and she says she would require her team to use it |
| It is worth €490 | M1 says yes to the price **before** being told what it costs |

**Any claim not on this list should not be made in marketing.**

---

## What would falsify it

Write these down too, or the test is theatre.

- Practitioners complete lessons but cannot name a behaviour change → the product is edutainment; the loop is not landing.
- P2 (the strong closer) finds nothing → reposition and reprice as junior training.
- P3 feels judged rather than taught → the feedback tone is wrong, and that is a content fix, not a UI fix.
- M1 reads the coaching centre as a surveillance tool → the framing is wrong and will lose clinics on referral.
- The Spanish tester switches to English at any point → parity is structural, not experiential, and the ES market is not ready.
- Nobody returns for Session 2 → the strongest possible signal, and the cheapest to get.

---

## Cost

Four people, roughly three hours each including the observed sessions. One week
elapsed. No incentive beyond access. This is small enough to run before the next
build decision, and it is the only thing that will tell you whether the next
build decision should be more content, better teaching, or a different price.
